## Companions and roles

Brand companion names describe colors; semantic role names describe jobs.

| Name | Kind | Intended use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Augur Navy | Brand anchor | Dark canvas and primary text on light surfaces |
| Augur Green | Brand accent | Intent and orientation signal on dark surfaces |
| Deep | Green companion | Accessible primary intent and focus signal on light surfaces |
| Wash | Green companion | Light green fill; not readable green text |
| Graphite | Supporting neutral | Secondary text on light surfaces |
| Pewter | Supporting neutral | Secondary text on dark surfaces |
| Mist | Navy companion | Control edges in both themes; dark rules and quiet marks; not readable copy |
| Muted | Light surface role | Quiet light regions |
| Surface 1 / 2 / 3 | Dark surface roles | Stepped tonal layers for panels, controls, and selected regions |

Do not substitute a companion name for a runtime job or vice versa. Components consume semantic roles rather than raw color tokens.

## Surfaces and depth

Depth is tonal, not ornamental.

Light UI uses Paper as the canvas, White for raised panels, Muted for quiet regions, and Border for hairlines. Dark UI uses Navy as the canvas, then Surface 1, Surface 2, and Surface 3 as stepped layers.

In this documentation, live-example previews sit on the quiet surface (Muted on light, Surface 1 on dark), so specimens read as placed on a region rather than floating on the canvas behind outlines.

There are no decorative shadows, glows, bevels, gradients, or textures in the default system.

## Semantic roles

Components and pages consume the shadcn-compatible semantic role layer: `--background`, `--foreground`, `--card`, `--primary`, `--muted`, `--border`, `--ring`, and related roles.

Two relationships are especially important:

- `--primary` resolves to Deep on light surfaces and Green on dark surfaces so primary intent remains readable in both themes.
- `--accent` is a neutral hover/highlight surface for outline, ghost, and menu controls, not the brand-green accent color. Generic hover should not multiply green signals. In dark it is Surface 3, one step above the canvas, cards, and popovers, so hover stays visible inside dialogs and menus.
- Filled buttons hover to a neighboring palette color in their own family through `--primary-hover`, `--secondary-hover`, and `--destructive-hover`, so an engaged primary never reads as disabled.
- `--input` (control edge) and `--border` (panel bound) are separate jobs: Mist and Border in light, Mist and Surface 3 in dark. Control edges step to `--input-hover` on hover.

The brand palette defines no dedicated danger hue. Destructive meaning must be explicit in the label and surrounding copy; color may reinforce it but cannot carry it alone.

See [Theming](/foundations/theming) for the full role-selection and scoping model.

## Signal hierarchy

Green should have a reason to be present.

- Usually choose one dominant green carrier per coherent task area.
- The primary action is the default carrier.
- Keyboard focus and a short orientation signal are justified additional uses.
- Do not add a green rule, success dot, or accent fill to every section.
- When a page's primary action carries the green, a record's short orientation rule on the same view steps down to a quiet rule (Graphite on light, Pewter on dark).
- On light surfaces, use Deep for readable green intent; raw Augur Green is reserved for dark-surface signal use. The one exception is the light primary-action hover: a transient Green fill under a Navy label that stays in the primary's own green family, so it adds no second signal.

## Contrast pairings

The package verifies the maintained pairings from generated values. Reference pairings include:

| Pairing | Recorded ratio | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Navy on Paper | 17.49:1 | Primary copy on the light canvas |
| Graphite on Paper | 7.81:1 | Secondary text on the light canvas |
| Deep on Paper | 7.17:1 | Readable green intent on light surfaces |
| Paper on Navy | 17.49:1 | Primary copy on the dark canvas |
| Pewter on Navy | 7.53:1 | Secondary text on the dark canvas |
| Paper on Surface 3 | 13.94:1 | Label on the dark hover/highlight surface |
| Pewter on Surface 3 | 6.00:1 | Secondary text on a raised dark step |
| Green on Navy | 11.87:1 | Green signal on dark surfaces; Navy label on the light primary hover |
| Navy on Wash | 17.15:1 | Label on the dark primary hover |
| White on Graphite | 8.50:1 | Label on the light destructive hover |
| Mist on Paper | 4.31:1 | Light control edge (non-text, 3:1 minimum) |
| Mist on Navy | 4.06:1 | Dark control edge (non-text, 3:1 minimum) |

New text pairings should meet WCAG AA or better. Meaningful non-text control boundaries and focus indicators must remain perceivable against adjacent surfaces. Hairline editorial separators should not be used as the sole carrier of meaning.

## Using color well

- Name state in words first; let color reinforce it.
- Keep primary copy stronger than supporting Graphite/Pewter text.
- Use Wash as a fill, not as a readable green foreground.
- Use surface steps to group content before inventing another border or shadow treatment.
- Treat green as a scarce intent/orientation signal rather than a general brand decoration.
